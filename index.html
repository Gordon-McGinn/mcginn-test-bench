Airframe

Model: 3-DOF longitudinal dynamics (x, z, pitch) with a blended post-stall lift curve, induced drag from aspect ratio, and thrust that falls off with airspeed. Touch down slower than 2.5 m/s sink or it counts as a crash.

Quad altitude

Your project's chain, simulated nonlinearly: PWM duty to motor voltage, to shaft speed, to thrust, to altitude. The drone starts in steady hover at 2 m, gets a 10 m climb command at t = 1 s, and has to hover again. Tune the PID and see what the step response does.

Vehicle

Motor constants are typical values for a 5-inch-class quad (Kt = Ke = 0.01, R = 0.15 Ω, J = 6×10⁻⁵ kg·m²), not your group's measured ones. Inductance is neglected, since its time constant is far shorter than the rotor's. The integrator starts out holding the hover duty and only accumulates within 1.5 m of the target, which keeps the long climb from winding it up.

Span loading

Prandtl lifting-line theory, solved as a Fourier series, gives the lift carried at every spanwise station. Integrating that load inboard from the tip gives the shear and bending moment the spar has to carry. The section's lift slope comes from the wind tunnel.

Flight condition

Defaults are your wireframe wing with the flap cut from 137.5 mm to 772.5 mm off the centreline. Flap effect is modelled as a 0.6° shift in zero-lift angle per degree of deflection, a typical figure for a 25 % chord plain flap. Spar loads ignore the wing's own weight.

Traction car

Your friction-measuring model car on a launch run. The motor spins the driven wheels, the tyre force follows a slip curve, and the two sensors you planned, a light-break wheel speed sensor and an accelerometer, are what recover the friction coefficient.

Run

Surface

Car

Surface values are ballpark figures. The estimate assumes you know the load on the driven axle: μ ≈ a / (g × driven-axle share). A slow PWM ramp passes through the peak of the curve, so the accelerometer catches it. On a slippery surface a hard launch jumps past the peak into wheelspin before the accelerometer can catch it, and reads low. Rolling resistance also pulls every estimate a few percent under the true value.

Op-amp bench

Three circuits from an electronics lab, with the non-ideal behaviour left in: finite gain-bandwidth, slew rate and supply rails. Push the gain, frequency or amplitude until the scope trace stops looking like the textbook answer.

Part figures are typical datasheet values. The output is assumed to swing to within 1.5 V of each rail. The integrator has R2 across the capacitor, as you would build it on a breadboard to stop it drifting into a rail.

DAQ sampler

What a logger records is not the signal, it is samples of the signal. Set a vibration frequency, a sample rate and an ADC resolution, and see what lands on the SD card, including the false frequency that appears when you sample too slowly.

Signal

Logger

Input range is 0 to 3.3 V, as on an ESP32 pin. The storage figure assumes three channels at two bytes each plus a four-byte timestamp per row, written as binary.

Engine cycles

    
Air-standard Otto, Diesel and Brayton cycles on P-v and T-s axes, with constant specific heats. Change the compression or pressure ratio and peak temperature and see the loop, the efficiency and the work output respond.

  

    

      

        
        

      

      

      

    

    

      
Cycle

      

      

      
Air as an ideal gas with k = 1.4, cp = 1.005 and cv = 0.718 kJ/kg·K, intake at 100 kPa. Real engines fall well short of these efficiencies because of heat loss, friction and variable specific heats.

    

  







  
Quench

    
A hot plate dropped into a coolant. Conduction inside the plate is solved by finite differences with convection at both faces, so you can see when the plate cools evenly and when the surface races ahead of the core.

One-dimensional conduction through the thickness with constant properties and a constant heat transfer coefficient. Boiling during a real water quench makes the coefficient change a great deal as the surface cools.
